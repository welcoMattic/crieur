Why we read slower than we listen

By Ada Lovecraft

Most of us read at about 250 words per minute, yet a podcast host speaks at 150. The numbers suggest that reading should win every time, and still millions of people listen to articles on their way to work.

The reason is not speed, it is attention. When you listen, your eyes are free: you can walk, cook or drive while a voice carries the argument. A long read that never fits in a busy day suddenly fits in a commute.

Voices that do not tire

Synthetic voices used to sound flat. In 2026, small models running on a laptop read with breath and rhythm, and they never stumble on the fifth paragraph. That changes what a reading list can be.

  • Articles become episodes.
  • Your reading list becomes a playlist.
The best article is the one you actually finish.

Of course, a voice cannot show a chart or a table. Good audio editions describe what matters and skip the rest, which is exactly what an attentive human reader would do.
